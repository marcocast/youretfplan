ETFs in 30 seconds

A basket of companiesAn ETF holds hundreds or thousands of companies at once, like Apple, Nestlé and Toyota. One purchase buys you a small slice of all of them.
You buy a bit every monthYou set up an automatic monthly payment, like a subscription. Some months you buy when prices are high, some when they are low. Over time it evens out.

What it really feels like

The numbers above are smooth averages. Real markets jump up and down, sometimes a lot. World stock markets fell by about half between 2007 and 2009, and by about a third in a few weeks in 2020. Both times they came back: within a few years after 2009, and within months in 2020.

How to start this week

  1. Open an account with a low-cost brokerA broker is the app or bank where you buy and hold ETFs. Pick one that is regulated in your country and lets you set up a monthly investment plan. Check how ETF taxes work where you live.
  2. Pick one world ETFLook for one that follows the whole world stock market, is accumulating and has a low yearly fee (TER), ideally under 0.25%. If you want fewer ups and downs, an all-in-one ETF that also holds bonds is fine too.
  3. Set up the monthly plan and leave it aloneUse the monthly amount from your plan above and a date right after payday. Then don't check it every day.

Questions everyone asks

Can I lose money?
Yes, in the short term, and sometimes for several years. Over 15 years or more, a world ETF has historically ended up higher, but that's not guaranteed. That's why you only invest money you won't need for a long time, and keep the emergency fund in the bank.
What if I need the money?
You can sell any day the market is open, and the cash arrives in a few days. But selling right after a drop locks in the loss. That's what the emergency fund is for.
Do I pay taxes?
Usually you pay tax only on your gains, and only when you sell. Rates and rules depend on your country, so check before you start. The results on this page are before tax.
When should I start?
As soon as your emergency fund is in place, even with a small amount. Time in the market matters more than picking the right moment, and nobody can reliably pick it.
Words explained
ETF
A fund you buy and sell on the stock exchange like a single share, but it holds many companies at once.
Accumulating
Dividends are not paid out to you. The fund reinvests them automatically, which is the simplest way to let the money grow.
Bonds
Loans to governments or companies that pay interest. They move less than stocks, so they calm a portfolio down, but on average they earn less.
Inflation
Prices going up over time. At 2% a year, something that costs €100 today will cost about €149 in 20 years.
TER
The yearly cost of an ETF, as a percentage. A TER of 0.2% on €10,000 is about €20 a year, taken out of the fund's value automatically.
Broker
The bank or app where you buy, hold and sell your ETFs.
