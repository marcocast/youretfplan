ETF taxes: what you pay when you sell

You don’t pay tax on everything. Only on what you gained. Put in what you paid and what it’s worth today: you see the tax and what you take home, in euros.

You put in €20,000. Today it’s €30,000. You pay tax only on the gain, €10,000: that’s €2,000. You take home €28,000.

Need only €5,000? You sell €5,000 of ETF. Only €1,667 of it is gain, so the tax is about €333. The rest stays invested.

Many countries tax gains between 0% and 40%. Put in yours. Some also tax a bit every year, even if you don’t sell: check before you start.

The rules, in plain words

  1. Usually you pay only on the gain, when you sell. Not on the whole amount, and not while you hold.
  2. Accumulating ETFs reinvest the dividends inside. In most countries that means no tax until you sell. Distributing ETFs pay you cash, and that’s taxed each time.
  3. A few countries tax a bit every year, even if you don’t sell. Check yours before you start.
  4. Ask who pays the tax. A local broker often does it for you. With a foreign one, you usually declare it yourself.
  5. Losses often lower tax on gains. The rules change a lot from country to country.

Questions

Do I pay tax every year?
On the gain, no: only when you sell, if the ETF is accumulating. In Italy you do pay the 0.2% stamp duty every year on what you hold. In Spain there’s nothing yearly on an accumulating ETF.
Accumulating or distributing?
Accumulating, if you don’t need the cash now. The dividends go back in by themselves and you pay tax only at the end. With a distributing ETF you pay tax on every payout: in Italy 26%, in Spain 19% is held back at once. What an ETF is, in 7 lessons →
If I never sell, do I never pay?
You pay when you sell. But waiting helps: the money you’d have paid in tax stays invested and keeps growing. One more reason to keep ETFs for money you won’t need for at least 5 years. The longer the better: over 15 years or more, historically, it has always gone well.
And the money in the bank, is it tax-free?
No. Interest on a bank deposit is taxed too: 26% in Italy, from 19% in Spain. And it’s taxed every year, not at the end.
Is the bank’s fund taxed differently?
Same tax rate on the gain. The difference is the yearly cost, often 1.5% or more, paid even in red years. In Spain a fund can be moved to another fund without paying (traspaso), an ETF can’t. ETF or the bank’s fund →
