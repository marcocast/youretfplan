How to buy your first ETF, step by step

From choosing a broker to your first automatic purchase. About an hour in total, no rush, no jargon. Tick each step when it’s done.

Your progress

0 of 6 steps done.

  1. 1Before you start: your safety cushion

    Keep about 6 months of expenses in the bank, in an account you can reach any day. It covers emergencies, so you never have to sell your ETFs at a bad moment.

    Only invest in ETFs money you won’t need for at least 10 years. If you don’t know your numbers yet, make your plan in 3 questions.

  2. 2Choose a broker

    A broker is the app or bank where you buy and hold your ETFs. Before choosing, check:

    • Regulated in the EU (or in your country): look it up on the regulator’s website.
    • Automatic monthly plans on ETFs, ideally with no fee per purchase.
    • No yearly account fee, or a small one.
    • How taxes work: whether the broker handles them for you or you declare them yourself.

    Your own bank can work too, but compare: banks often charge more per purchase and a yearly fee.

    What a fee per purchase really costs

    €2.00

    Fee: 2% of each purchase, about €24 a year. A world ETF’s yearly cost (TER) is about 0.2%: here the fee costs much more than the ETF itself. Look for a broker with free monthly plans.

  3. 3Open the account

    It takes 10–20 minutes on your phone. You’ll need:

    • An ID document (ID card or passport) and sometimes a selfie.
    • Your tax number.
    • A bank account (IBAN) in your name, to send money in and out.

    The broker will ask a few questions about your experience. It’s required by EU rules: answer honestly, it’s fine to be a beginner. The account is usually ready in 1–3 days.

  4. 4Find your ETF

    You don’t need the “best” ETF, just a good one. Look for:

    • It follows a world index, such as MSCI World, MSCI ACWI or FTSE All-World: thousands of companies in one go.
    • Accumulating (“Acc”): dividends are reinvested for you.
    • Low yearly cost (TER), ideally under 0.25%.
    • Large: hundreds of millions in the fund or more.

    Every ETF has an ISIN, a 12-character code like a number plate. Write it down and search for it in your broker’s app, so you buy exactly that one. Before buying, open its KID, the short official sheet with costs and risks.

    These are criteria, not a recommendation of a specific ETF: compare a few before you choose.

  5. 5Set up the monthly plan

    In the app, choose the ETF, then “savings plan” or “recurring investment”:

    • Amount: the monthly amount from your plan.
    • Day: right after payday, so the money is always there.
    • Send money to the broker, or set a monthly transfer from your bank.

    On the chosen day the broker buys automatically. You’ll often own fractions of a share: that’s normal and how small amounts work.

  6. 6Then: leave it alone

    In the app you’ll see the value in euros and a + or − percentage. Some months it will be red: that’s normal, and with a monthly plan you’re buying at a discount.

    • Don’t check it every day. Once every few months is enough.
    • Once a year: raise the monthly amount if you can.
    • Don’t sell in a crash. Selling after a fall turns a temporary drop into a real loss.

Taxes and accounts to check in your country

Usually you pay tax only on gains, and only when you sell. Some brokers handle taxes for you; with others you declare them yourself. Rules depend on your country: check before you choose a broker.

Taxes change, and they vary. Tax rules change often, sometimes every year, and depend on your country, your region and your own situation. What you read here is a general summary, checked in October 2026: always check the current rules with an accountant or tax adviser before deciding.

Questions

Can I use my own bank?
Yes, if it offers ETFs and monthly plans. Compare the costs: many banks charge a yearly account fee and a fee per purchase that, on small monthly amounts, costs more than the ETF itself.
What if I pick the wrong ETF?
With a broad world ETF that’s cheap and large, there’s no really wrong choice: they hold much the same companies. Picking one and starting matters more than finding the perfect one.
Do I have to buy every month?
No. You can pause, change or stop the plan whenever you want, and sell whenever the market is open. Buying every month simply makes it automatic and evens out the ups and downs.
